title: Trades and committee industries
description: How Term notes a stock trade in an industry one of the member's committees oversees, and the full list of what each committee is taken to oversee.
---

Some trades on a member's Financial disclosures tab carry a note: **"In an industry the House Committee on Armed Services oversees"**. This page explains what that note means and what it does not.

## What the note means

The note shows that the company traded is in an industry a committee the member sits on oversees, under the list on this page. That is all it says. It is a note of overlap, **not a finding of a conflict of interest or of any wrongdoing**. Members are allowed to trade stocks, and most of what a committee oversees is not decided by any one member.

## How it is decided

1. **The company's industry** comes from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. Each listed company has a four-digit Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) code, matched here through the ticker on the trade report.
2. **What each committee oversees** comes from the list below. Each committee's core industries are given as ranges of SIC codes, based on the committee's jurisdiction under House Rule X and Senate Rule XXV.
3. A trade gets the note when the member sat on that committee in the Congress of the trade, and the company's code falls in one of that committee's ranges.

## What it leaves out

- **The list is deliberately narrow.** It names a committee's core industries only. Armed Services covers weapons makers, not every company that sells to the Pentagon. Committees whose reach is the whole economy (Appropriations, Budget, Ways and Means, Finance, the Judiciary committees and the oversight committees) are not mapped at all, because a note on nearly every trade would tell a reader nothing. So the note **undercounts**: some real overlaps are not noted.
- **Some defense contractors carry no note.** Aircraft and aircraft-parts makers are not mapped to the Armed Services committees, because most of their business is commercial aviation. Contractors the SEC classes under missiles, space vehicles or defense electronics are mapped.
- **Food makers other than meat and poultry packers are not mapped.**
- **Only companies with a ticker the SEC lists have an industry.** Bonds, Treasury and municipal debt, funds and private holdings never get the note.
- **Subcommittees are not mapped.**
- **Committee seats have no dates in the source.** A member who changed committees during a Congress is matched on the committees recorded for that Congress.

The list is reviewed at the start of each Congress. If you think a line is wrong, the list below is exactly what the site uses, so a correction is a change to one line.
